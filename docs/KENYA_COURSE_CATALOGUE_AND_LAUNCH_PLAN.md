# SkillForge Academy: Kenya Course Catalogue & 30-Day Launch Plan

## Positioning

**Learn a skill. Build something real. Put it to work.** SkillForge serves young learners who want job-ready portfolio projects and business owners who want practical digital skills. Sell outcomes and projects rather than vague promises. Never promise employment or income.

## Demand signals to guide, not guarantee, the decision

- Upwork's 2026 skills report reported rising demand for AI-related work on its own marketplace, including AI integration and AI video generation/editing. These are marketplace-specific indicators, not Kenyan job-growth estimates: https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upworks-demand-skills-2026-demand-top-ai-skills-more-doubles-ai
- A Kenyan digital-skills needs assessment identified gaps including digital marketing, data analytics, cybersecurity and automation: https://jointsdgfund.org/publication/new-needs-assessment-maps-kenyas-digital-skills-gap-msms-and-marginalized-communities
- Kenya's ICT ministry describes national digital-skills efforts: https://ict.go.ke/digital-skills

These sources justify testing the subjects; they do not prove that a specific SkillForge course will sell. Use paid conversions and learner completion to validate local demand.

## Recommended first launch

Launch these three first:
1. AI Tools and Automation for Business — KSh 999.
2. Build Websites and Landing Pages — KSh 1,499.
3. Excel, Business Data and Dashboards — KSh 999.

Keep production focused. Make sure each course has concise lessons, one free preview where appropriate, a practical capstone, clear prerequisites and a downloadable project brief. Add a video-editing course promotion after the first launch has useful evidence.

## 30-day plan

### Days 1–7: Prepare
- Review the first three courses for lesson quality, accuracy and working project instructions.
- Publish free previews and course outcomes.
- Verify mobile usability, checkout status, receipts, course access after verified payment, support contact and refund terms.
- Do not describe payment integrations as live until a real end-to-end transaction has been tested.

### Days 8–14: Pre-sell
- Publish short project demonstrations on WhatsApp Status, TikTok, Facebook, LinkedIn and relevant communities.
- Contact local small businesses, student groups, youth organisations and community training groups.
- Offer a time-limited founding price only if the end date and terms are clear.
- Initial target: 10–20 paid learners, treated as a target rather than a forecast.

### Days 15–21: Deliver
- Help learners finish their project and ask for specific feedback.
- Request explicit permission before publishing student work or testimonials.
- Record common questions, lesson completion and support time.

### Days 22–30: Optimise
- Promote the course with the best paid conversion and completion, not merely the most views.
- Test a KSh 2,499 bundle and a small business group package.
- Pause or revise offers that attract clicks but not purchases.

## Metrics to review weekly

- Qualified visitors to checkout starts.
- Paid purchases by course and marketing channel.
- Refunds and payment failures.
- Lesson-one starts and course completion.
- Capstone submission rate.
- Learner satisfaction and support requests.
- Net revenue after payment fees, advertising, refunds, taxes and delivery costs.

## Important trust rules

- Never invent enrolment counts, ratings, testimonials, provider verification or learner outcomes.
- Clearly label all sample datasets and demonstrations; do not present sample figures as real business results.
- Grant course access only after payment confirmation from the configured provider or verified payment webhook.
- Never ask learners to share their M-Pesa PIN or passwords.
- Explain what the certificate represents and never imply it guarantees employment or income.
